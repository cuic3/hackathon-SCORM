Health & Veterinary Care

Vaccinations & Spay/Neuter

Kittens typically start a core vaccination series (including rabies and FVRCP) around 6-8 weeks old, with boosters every few weeks until about 16 weeks, then a booster schedule set by a veterinarian afterward.

Spaying or neutering, usually recommended around 4-6 months of age, prevents unwanted litters and is linked to lower rates of certain cancers and behavioral issues such as roaming and urine marking.

Annual Vet Visits

Cats hide illness well — a wild-animal instinct to not appear weak in front of predators, which doesn't disappear just because they live on a couch. An annual (or twice-yearly for seniors) wellness exam is often the only way early disease, like kidney or thyroid issues, gets caught before it becomes serious.

Warning Signs That Need a Vet, Not a Wait-and-See

  • Not eating or drinking for more than 24 hours
  • Straining in the litter box or crying while trying to urinate
  • Hiding much more than usual, especially combined with lethargy
  • Repeated vomiting or diarrhea lasting more than a day
  • Labored or open-mouth breathing
Indoor safety matters too: unscreened open windows ("high-rise syndrome"), toxic houseplants like lilies, and small ingestible objects (string, rubber bands, hair ties) are among the most common preventable emergency vet visits for otherwise healthy indoor cats.
