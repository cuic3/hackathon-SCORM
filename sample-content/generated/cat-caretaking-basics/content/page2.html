Litter Box & Grooming

The Litter Box Setup

Litter box problems are the #1 behavioral reason cats are surrendered to shelters — and most of them come down to setup, not the cat "being bad."

  • The "n+1" rule: provide one more litter box than you have cats. One cat, two boxes. Two cats, three boxes.
  • Location: quiet, easily accessible, and never right next to a loud appliance like a washing machine — cats can develop a lasting aversion to a box after being startled in it once.
  • Scooping: at least once a day. Cats are fastidious and a dirty box is a common reason a cat starts eliminating elsewhere in the house.
A sudden change in litter box habits (straining, going outside the box, frequent small trips) is one of the most reliable early warning signs of a urinary blockage, especially in male cats — this can become life-threatening within 24-48 hours and warrants an urgent vet visit, not a "wait and see."

Grooming Basics

  • Brushing: weekly for short-haired cats, several times a week for long-haired breeds, to reduce shedding and hairball frequency.
  • Nail trims: every 2-3 weeks if a cat doesn't self-maintain enough through scratching.
  • Dental care: dental disease is extremely common in adult cats; regular brushing or dental treats/diets help, and a vet should check teeth at routine visits.
