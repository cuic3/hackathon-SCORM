Feeding & Nutrition

Cats are obligate carnivores, meaning their bodies are built to run on animal protein specifically, not just protein in general. A diet formulated for cats (not dogs, and not table scraps) is the single biggest lever an owner has over a cat's long-term health.

How Often to Feed

  • Kittens (under 1 year): 3-4 small meals a day — their energy needs per pound of body weight are much higher than an adult's.
  • Adult cats: 2 measured meals a day is typical. Free-feeding dry food all day is a common cause of feline obesity.
  • Fresh water: available at all times, changed daily. Cats are prone to urinary tract issues, and adequate water intake is one of the simplest preventive measures.
Measuring food by volume (a scoop) rather than by the bag's recommended weight is one of the most common reasons cats become overweight — "a scoop" varies a lot depending on the scoop.

Foods That Are Toxic to Cats

Several common human foods are genuinely dangerous, not just "not recommended":

  • Chocolate (theobromine toxicity)
  • Onions, garlic, and other allium vegetables (damage red blood cells)
  • Grapes and raisins (linked to kidney injury in cats and dogs)
  • Lily plants — not a food, but even small amounts of pollen or leaf ingested from true lilies (Lilium species) can cause acute kidney failure in cats and are a genuine veterinary emergency
