The Five Rights of Medication Administration

Medication errors are among the most common preventable causes of harm in healthcare. The "Five Rights" is a foundational checklist taught to every clinician who administers medications, meant to be verified before every single dose, every time, with no exceptions for busy shifts.

  1. Right patient — verify identity using at least two identifiers (for example, name and date of birth), not room number or a general glance.
  2. Right medication — confirm the drug matches the order, watching especially for look-alike/sound-alike drug names.
  3. Right dose — confirm the dose is appropriate for this patient, and double-check any calculation, especially for weight-based or pediatric dosing.
  4. Right route — confirm the medication is given by the intended route (oral, IV, IM, topical, etc.), since the same drug can behave very differently, or be dangerous, by the wrong route.
  5. Right time — confirm the dose is given at the correct time relative to the order and any timing-sensitive requirements (before meals, spaced from another drug, etc.).
Many organizations now teach an expanded list that also includes right documentation and right reason (confirming the indication still applies) — but the original five remain the non-negotiable core check before any medication reaches a patient.

Even experienced clinicians benefit from treating these checks as a fixed ritual rather than something to skip when confident. Most serious medication errors happen when a rushed or distracted clinician assumes they already know the answer to one of these five questions.
