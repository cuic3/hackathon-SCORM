High-Alert Medications and Double-Checks

Some medications carry a heightened risk of causing serious harm if used in error, even though the mistake itself may be no different from an error with a lower-risk drug. These are known as "high-alert medications," and they warrant extra safeguards.

Common High-Alert Categories

  • Insulin
  • Anticoagulants (for example, heparin, warfarin)
  • Opioids and other sedatives
  • Concentrated electrolytes (for example, potassium chloride)
  • Chemotherapy agents
  • Neuromuscular blocking agents

An error with any of these drugs is more likely to cause serious harm than an equivalent-sized error with a lower-risk medication, which is why they receive extra layers of protection in most healthcare organizations.

Independent Double-Checks

For high-alert medications, many organizations require an independent double-check: a second qualified clinician verifies the order, the calculation, the pump settings, and the patient identity separately, without being told the first clinician's answer in advance. This independence is what makes the check effective — if the second person is only confirming what they're told, shared assumptions can slip through unnoticed.

A true independent double-check means the second clinician performs their own calculation and comparison from scratch, not simply nodding along to what the first person read aloud.
