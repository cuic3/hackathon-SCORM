Common Causes of Medication Errors

Medication errors rarely happen because a clinician doesn't know the rules. They happen because of predictable system and human-factors pressures that build up during a normal shift. Recognizing these patterns helps you guard against them.

Interruptions and Distractions

Being interrupted mid-task while preparing or administering medication is one of the strongest predictors of error. Many hospitals now use "do not interrupt" vests or designated quiet zones for medication preparation for exactly this reason.

Look-Alike, Sound-Alike Drugs

Drug names that look or sound similar on paper or in verbal orders (for example, hydroxyzine and hydralazine) are a well-documented source of mix-ups. Reading the full name carefully, and questioning anything ambiguous, is essential.

Calculation Errors

Weight-based dosing, unit conversions (for example, milligrams versus micrograms), and infusion rate calculations are common places where a decimal point or unit error can cause a significant overdose or underdose. Independent double-checks for high-risk calculations catch many of these before they reach the patient.

Workarounds and Shortcuts

When a process feels slow or redundant, it can be tempting to skip a barcode scan or a second signature "just this once." These workarounds are exactly the safety checks designed to catch the rare but serious error, and they only work if used every time.

A "just this once" skip is invisible until the one time it would have caught a real error. Consistency, not judgment calls in the moment, is what makes medication safety systems work.
