What to Document and When

Infection control documentation generally falls into a few key categories. Knowing what belongs in each, and documenting it as close to real time as possible, keeps the record trustworthy.

At Initiation of Precautions

  • The specific type of precaution started (contact, droplet, airborne, etc.)
  • The clinical or lab reason precautions were started
  • The date and time precautions began
  • Signage placed and patient/family education provided

During Ongoing Care

  • Any changes in the patient's infectious status
  • Confirmation that precautions were maintained during each shift
  • Any breach in precautions and the corrective action taken

At Discontinuation

  • The date, time, and clinical or lab reason precautions were discontinued
  • Terminal cleaning performed for the room, if applicable
Document at the time care is given, not from memory at the end of a shift. Retrospective documentation is more likely to be incomplete or inaccurate, especially on a busy day.

Timeliness matters as much as completeness: a precaution that isn't documented until hours after it was started leaves a dangerous gap where other staff may not know to take the necessary precautions.
