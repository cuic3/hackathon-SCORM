Standard Precautions and Isolation Documentation

Standard precautions apply to every patient, every time, regardless of known infectious status, and generally do not require special documentation beyond routine care notes. Transmission-based (isolation) precautions, however, are added on top of standard precautions for specific patients and must be clearly and specifically documented.

The Three Transmission-Based Categories

  • Contact precautions — for organisms spread by direct or indirect contact (for example, certain multidrug-resistant organisms). Requires gown and gloves for room entry.
  • Droplet precautions — for organisms spread by respiratory droplets over short distances (for example, influenza). Requires a surgical mask for close patient contact.
  • Airborne precautions — for organisms that remain suspended in air over longer distances (for example, tuberculosis, measles). Requires a fit-tested N95 or equivalent respirator and, typically, a negative-pressure room.

Each of these must be documented with the specific category used (not just "isolation"), because the required personal protective equipment and room requirements differ significantly between them. Documenting only "isolation precautions" without specifying which type leaves the next clinician guessing about what protective equipment to use.

Be specific: document the precaution category, the organism or clinical reason if known, and the date/time started. Vague documentation is nearly as risky as no documentation at all.
