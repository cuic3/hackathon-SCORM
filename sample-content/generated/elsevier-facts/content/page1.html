Where the Name Comes From

Elsevier's name isn't invented from scratch — it's a tribute to a real family of publishers who lived centuries before the modern company existed.

The Elzevir Family

The Elzevir family were renowned Dutch publishers and booksellers, active mainly in the 16th and 17th centuries. They became famous across Europe for producing small, high-quality, portable editions of classical texts — at a time when most books were large, expensive, and hard to carry around.

Choosing this name when the modern company was founded was a deliberate nod to that legacy: careful, high-quality publishing, made accessible.

Founded in 1880

The modern Elsevier company was established in 1880 in Rotterdam, the Netherlands — long after the historical Elzevir family itself, but carrying their name forward into a new era of scientific and scholarly publishing.
