Recognizing the Warning Signs

Two widely used screening approaches help clinicians flag patients who may be developing sepsis: SIRS criteria and qSOFA (quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment).

SIRS Criteria (Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome)

A patient meeting two or more of the following may be showing a systemic inflammatory response:

  • Temperature above 38°C (100.4°F) or below 36°C (96.8°F)
  • Heart rate above 90 beats per minute
  • Respiratory rate above 20 breaths per minute
  • Abnormal white blood cell count (very high, very low, or a high proportion of immature forms)

qSOFA (Bedside Screening)

qSOFA can be assessed quickly at the bedside without waiting on lab results. A score of 2 or more of the following suggests a higher risk of poor outcome:

  • Altered mental status (any new confusion)
  • Respiratory rate of 22 breaths per minute or greater
  • Systolic blood pressure of 100 mmHg or less
No single vital sign confirms sepsis on its own. What matters is noticing a cluster of abnormal findings, especially a change from the patient's own baseline, and escalating promptly rather than waiting to see if the patient "looks sicker" later.

Other Red Flags

Mottled or clammy skin, unusually low urine output, extreme pain or discomfort disproportionate to exam findings, and a general sense from experienced staff that "something is wrong" with the patient are all reasons to escalate and screen for sepsis, even if formal criteria are borderline.
