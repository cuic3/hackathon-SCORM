The Sepsis Six and Time-Critical Response

Once sepsis is suspected, treatment is time-critical. The "Sepsis Six" is a bundle of actions widely taught to frontline staff, intended to be started within the first hour of recognition:

  1. Give high-flow oxygen if indicated.
  2. Take blood cultures (and consider other cultures based on suspected source).
  3. Give intravenous antibiotics, per local protocol, as soon as possible.
  4. Give intravenous fluids to support blood pressure and perfusion.
  5. Check lactate level, a key marker of tissue hypoperfusion.
  6. Monitor urine output closely.
The phrase used in sepsis education is "time is tissue." Delayed antibiotics and fluids are strongly associated with worse outcomes, which is why early recognition by any member of the care team — not only physicians — is emphasized so heavily.

Your Role in Early Recognition

You do not need to diagnose sepsis to save a life. If you notice a patient who seems more confused, feels unusually unwell, has a fast heart rate or breathing rate, or simply "doesn't look right," escalate immediately using your unit's rapid response or early warning score process. Early escalation, even if it turns out not to be sepsis, is always the safer choice.
