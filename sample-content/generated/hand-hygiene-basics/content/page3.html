Proper Handwashing Technique

Knowing when to perform hand hygiene is only half the picture — doing it correctly and for long enough is what actually removes or kills pathogens on the skin.

Alcohol-Based Hand Rub (20-30 seconds)

  1. Apply enough product to cover all surfaces of both hands.
  2. Rub palm to palm.
  3. Rub the back of each hand with the palm of the other, fingers interlaced.
  4. Rub palm to palm with fingers interlaced.
  5. Rub the backs of fingers against opposing palms with fingers interlocked.
  6. Rub each thumb clasped in the opposite hand, using a rotating motion.
  7. Rub fingertips of each hand in the opposite palm, using a circular motion.
  8. Continue until hands are completely dry — do not wipe off product early.

Soap and Water (40-60 seconds)

  1. Wet hands with water and apply enough soap to cover all surfaces.
  2. Rub hands together using the same palm, back-of-hand, interlaced-finger, thumb, and fingertip motions as above.
  3. Rinse hands thoroughly with water.
  4. Dry hands thoroughly with a single-use towel.
  5. Use the towel to turn off the tap, avoiding recontaminating clean hands.
Common mistakes that reduce effectiveness: rushing (under 15 seconds), missing the thumbs and fingertips, wearing rings or long nails that trap organisms, and applying hand rub to hands that are visibly soiled instead of washing with soap and water first.
