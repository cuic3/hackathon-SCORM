The 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene

The World Health Organization's "My 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene" framework gives every clinician a simple, memorable way to know exactly when hand hygiene is required during patient care, regardless of unit or specialty.

  1. Before touching a patient — protects the patient from germs on your hands.
  2. Before a clean or aseptic procedure — protects the patient from harmful germs, including their own, entering their body during procedures such as inserting a line or dressing a wound.
  3. After body fluid exposure risk — protects you and the care environment from potentially harmful germs after any task with a risk of exposure to blood or body fluids.
  4. After touching a patient — protects you and the care environment from germs the patient may be carrying.
  5. After touching a patient's surroundings — protects you and the care environment from germs picked up from surfaces or objects in the patient's immediate vicinity, even without directly touching the patient.
A useful way to remember this: think of hand hygiene as a boundary you reset every time you cross into or out of a patient's personal zone, not just something you do "sometimes" during a shift.

Why Moments, Not Just Frequency

Counting how many times a shift someone washes their hands is a poor proxy for safety. What actually interrupts transmission chains is performing hand hygiene at the correct moments relative to patient contact. A clinician who washes hands ten times a shift but skips the moment right before an aseptic procedure has left the patient exposed at the highest-risk point.
